McDonald's: A Journey Through Time

Discover how a small burger stand became the world’s largest fast-food empire.

1940 - The Beginning

In 1940, brothers Richard and Maurice McDonald opened "McDonald's Bar-B-Q" in San Bernardino, California. It was a typical drive-in restaurant with carhops serving customers. The restaurant had a vast menu of 25 items, including barbecue, but the most popular items were hamburgers, fries, and milkshakes.

After analyzing their sales, the McDonald brothers realized that a few menu items generated most of their revenue. To maximize efficiency and profits, they decided to streamline the menu, focusing solely on fast-service hamburgers, fries, and drinks.

In 1948, they closed the original restaurant for three months to redesign their business model, pioneering what would later become the fast-food industry.

1948 - The Speedee Service System

The McDonald brothers introduced the **"Speedee Service System"** in 1948, revolutionizing the restaurant industry. They applied assembly-line principles to food preparation, reducing waiting times and ensuring consistent quality.

Customers placed their orders at a counter instead of relying on carhops. The brothers also removed unnecessary menu items, focusing on just **hamburgers, cheeseburgers, fries, shakes, and sodas**. This allowed them to serve food much faster than traditional diners.

To promote their efficiency, McDonald's introduced their first-ever mascot, **Speedee,** a smiling chef with a hamburger for a head, symbolizing their commitment to quick service.

1954 - Ray Kroc Joins McDonald's

Ray Kroc, a milkshake machine salesman, was intrigued when he noticed that the McDonald brothers had purchased eight of his **Multimixer milkshake machines.** This indicated an unusually high sales volume for a single restaurant.

When he visited their establishment in San Bernardino, he was amazed by the efficiency of their system and the potential for nationwide expansion. He approached the McDonald brothers with a proposal to **franchise their restaurant model across the United States.**

By 1955, Ray Kroc opened the first **McDonald's franchise in Des Plaines, Illinois.** He aggressively expanded the business, eventually purchasing the company from the brothers for **$2.7 million in 1961.**

1968 - The Birth of the Big Mac

One of the most iconic McDonald's menu items, the **Big Mac,** was introduced in 1968. Created by franchisee Jim Delligatti, it was designed to compete with larger sandwiches offered by competitors.

The Big Mac featured **two beef patties, special sauce, lettuce, cheese, pickles, and onions** on a three-part sesame seed bun. Initially sold for just 45 cents, it quickly became a customer favorite.

Today, the Big Mac is **one of McDonald's best-selling items worldwide,** with an estimated **900 million Big Macs sold annually.**

1990 - Expansion to Russia

In 1990, McDonald's opened its first location in **Moscow, Russia,** marking a historic moment as **the first American fast-food chain to enter the Soviet Union.** The restaurant broke records on its first day, serving over **30,000 customers.**

The Moscow McDonald's symbolized cultural change, as it introduced Western capitalism to the Soviet public. The **line to enter stretched for blocks,** showing the enormous demand for American-style fast food.

Today, McDonald's has adapted its menu to local cultures in over **100 countries,** with more than **38,000 locations worldwide.**
